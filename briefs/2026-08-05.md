## 1. Tape

Wall Street closed at record highs Tuesday on a four-day rally: S&P 500 +1.79% to 7,736.52, Nasdaq +2.59% to 26,584.99, Dow +1.71% to 54,085.88, with tech +4%, industrials and materials up ~2% each. Two things are driving it. First, geopolitics: Treasury Secretary Bessent said the US is in talks with Iran and a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could land within days, which knocked WTI down over 6% to $75.32 and pulled inflation risk out of the tape. Second, earnings: the latest batch pointed to continued AI build-out demand. Overnight, Trump said the Strait will reopen "very soon" and a 60-day interim US–Iran–Oman arrangement is close; oil slipped again.

The macro cross-current just landed. July ADP printed +44,000 versus a 75,000 consensus, with June revised down to 95,000. In a regime where the July FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% on a 9–3 vote with three dissents favouring a *hike*, a soft labour print is net-dovish and supportive of duration and equities — but it also puts more weight on ISM Services at 10:00 ET and makes Friday's payrolls the week's real event.

My read: **today favours selective trading, not index exposure.** Vol is compressed (VIX prior close 16.50), the index is extended into its fifth up-day, and there is a 10:00 ET data event that can reverse the open. But the single-stock dispersion from this earnings batch is unusually wide and produces two clean, dated, verifiable setups. I want stock-specific risk, not beta.
