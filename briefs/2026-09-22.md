## 1. Tape

Monday was a genuine risk-on impulse with two independent legs. The first was AI: Meta's "Muse" personal agent, released under two weeks ago, has topped its iOS category for downloads, and the read-through to inference demand sent Meta +11.3%, with Intel and AMD each up over 9%. The S&P 500 closed +1.49% at 7,764.70 and the Nasdaq Composite +2.26% at 27,122.09 — the best day since early August and a record Nasdaq close. The second leg was oil: Brent settled −3.4% near $100 for a fourth straight down session, and October WTI fell ~4.5%, on hopes that UN General Assembly high-level week in New York produces movement on the Iran war, plus satellite data showing Saudi Persian Gulf loadings at their highest since June as the kingdom routes around its drone-damaged East-West pipeline.

This morning those two legs have separated. Crude is down a further ~2.3%, dragging energy equities (−1.3%) and oil stocks (−1.6%), while semis are giving back ~1.0% of Monday's surge. Leadership has rotated to nuclear (+1.0%), homebuilders (+1.0%) and retail (+0.9%), and IWM is out in front (+0.5% to +0.8% across the reads I pulled) while QQQ sits roughly flat. That is a rotation day, not a continuation day.

The regime that matters is the one underneath: this is an energy-shock tape with a **hiking** Fed. Kevin Warsh's FOMC raised to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September, the September SEP marked 2026 year-end PCE up a full point to 3.7%, and futures price ~4.2% by December. The 10-year closed at 4.96%, the 30-year at 5.29%, the dollar index at 100.37. Against that, the S&P is at a record with the VIX at 14.87.

**My read: today does not favour putting on new risk.** The market has rallied four sessions on a diplomatic outcome that does not exist yet — there is no ceasefire in force, no scheduled Trump–Pezeshkian meeting, and Iran's parliament has publicly called negotiations futile pending three conditions relayed via Qatar. The actual event, Pezeshkian's General Assembly address, is *tomorrow*. Meanwhile Williams and Jefferson speak 35 and 50 minutes after the open, in a cycle where Williams has already signalled openness to hiking. A record-high index at VIX 15 into a two-way geopolitical headline and two senior Fed speakers is a place to manage existing risk, not add new risk.

## 3. Open positions review

**DAL — long 457 @ $80.38, last $84.68, +$1,963 (+5.3%). Thesis: INTACT. Action: hold, install a live stop, keep the original target.**

The thesis was that DAL had been marked down ~17% from its $95.68 52-week high on the Iran-driven jet fuel shock and would re-rate as that premium bled out. It is working, and for the right reason: crude has now fallen five consecutive sessions, and the driver is not purely headline — Saudi Persian Gulf loadings are verifiably back to their highest since June. DAL traded $82.50 Monday (+3.6%) and $84.01–$84.68 this morning.

Two things need saying honestly.

First, **this position has been running with no live stop order.** The book shows $78.20 marked "no live order." That is a standing breach of risk rule 6 and it is the single most valuable thing I can fix this session. I am installing a live stop at **$82.40**. That sits just below Monday's $82.50 close — the origin of the de-escalation move — so it only triggers if the entire Monday impulse is given back, which would be a real thesis invalidation rather than noise. It locks a minimum of $2.02/share, about +$923. The position can no longer produce a loss.

Second, **I am not moving the target.** The plan said $84.90 and price is 22 cents away; with crude down another 2.3% today it will very likely fill on the open. Analyst targets sit near $100 and the 52-week high is $95.68, so there is an argument for extending — but extending a target on a winner *after* the move has gone my way, on the day it is about to fill, is precisely the post-hoc revision this log exists to prevent. I take the trade as written. If I want more airline beta after it closes, that is a new trade with its own brief.

I considered whether to close at market instead. No: the resting limit 22 cents away is strictly better than selling here, and the stop now caps the downside.

## 4. New plays

**No new positions today.**

I looked hard and could not find a setup worth 1% of equity. The reasoning, so it can be checked later:

- **The dominant driver is a binary I cannot handicap.** Everything moving this tape — oil, airlines, energy, the risk-on bid — traces to whether UNGA week produces Iran de-escalation. There is no agreement in force, no scheduled leaders' meeting, and Tehran's parliament says talks are futile. Four sessions of rally have already priced the optimistic branch. Taking either side today, the day *before* Pezeshkian speaks, is a coin flip on a headline, not a setup.
- **I am already long this theme and near the sector cap.** DAL is 38% of equity against a 40% single-sector limit, and it is the levered expression of exactly this trade. Adding another oil-beneficiary before it closes is concentration dressed up as diversification.
- **Two Fed speakers land 35 minutes after the open in a hiking cycle.** With the 10-year at 4.96% and a hawkish chair, a firm Williams headline at 16:05 Stockholm is a live downside risk to a record-high index at VIX 14.87. I would rather see that clear.
- **I could not verify intraday levels cleanly enough to place entries.** The premarket screens I pulled returned prints I do not believe (WST −26%, MOH −25%, LGND −30%) with no corroborating news. I will not build a stop off a quote I cannot stand behind.

**What would change my mind:** a confirmed Trump–Pezeshkian meeting or a concrete ceasefire framework out of UNGA (I would want long transports/consumer and short energy, sized after DAL closes); or the mirror image — talks visibly collapsing, which would make energy and crude the trade in the other direction. Either resolves the binary and gives me something to price. Absent that, a hawkish Fed-speaker-driven flush that pulls semis back to a defined level would also be workable.
